# Professor Guide

Professors create their own CoolHack username and password, then create and run
their own classes. No professor email address, administrator code, or approval
is required. Database policies keep each professor's classes separate.

## Release one weekly scenario

Every scenario begins hidden. In the Professor Dashboard, find the class under
**Sections, teams, and mission progress**, choose the week's scenario, and
select **Reveal selected scenario**. Select **Hide scenario** when students
should no longer open it.

Only one scenario is visible to students in the class at a time. Changing the
released scenario moves every team in that class to the new workspace without
deleting work saved for an earlier scenario.

For every released scenario, the dashboard displays an AI security coaching
guide with an opening speech, discussion prompts, and the takeaway students
should be able to explain in an interview. Scenario 1 also includes the private
incident answer guide.

## Create an account

1. Open the professor sign-in route:
   `https://YOUR-PAGES-URL/?portal=professor#classroom-access`
2. Select **First visit: create account**.
3. Choose a CoolHack-only username that does not contain an institutional email
   address, employee ID, or other personal identifier.
4. Create and confirm a unique password of at least 12 characters.
5. Save the password in a password manager.
6. Select **Create professor account**.
7. In the dashboard, create the class and copy its student class link.

Never send the password to the administrator or students.

## Create and archive classes

Use **Create my class** for each class you teach. Names must be unique among
your active classes. You can archive your own class without deleting its saved
work. You cannot view, change, or archive another professor's class.

## Prepare the section

After signing in:

1. Create or select your class.
2. Copy its class link and post it in Canvas.
3. Tell one student in each four-person group to create the team and choose a
   neutral team name.
4. The other three students open the same link, select the team name, and join.
5. After all four invented aliases appear, verify the roster and assign one
   distinct role to each student.
6. Open the intended mission and confirm its lock state.

Do not collect real names, institutional emails, IDs, grades, or personal
details inside CoolHack.

## Run a mission

1. Confirm that the first student created the intended team and the other three
   selected it from the class list.
2. Require every student to use an invented screen name and a unique
   CoolHack-only password.
3. Confirm that all four roles are assigned.
4. Ask the team to read the five-item **Concept Refresh** aloud and connect the
   terms to the current case.
5. Require all four roles to select an artifact and record an individual
   finding and proposed action. CoolHack keeps the AI supervisor locked until
   every role contributes.
6. Require the three-question coaching check. Its immediate explanations are
   for revision, not punitive grading; students may correct an answer.
7. Ask the team to agree on and record its initial decision before opening AI.
8. One student operates a permitted AI tool while sharing the screen. Rotate
   both the workplace roles and the AI operator in later missions.
9. The team stores one shared AI transcript and one shared report.
10. Require the team to explain **What changed—and why?** after the AI
    conversation. A change is not automatically good; it must be justified by
    an artifact or corrected reasoning.
11. Ask one student to deliver the **60-second SOC shift briefing** aloud:
    situation, strongest evidence, action, remaining risk, and next owner.
12. Require the five-sentence **AI Security Brief**: risk, affected asset,
   evidence, control, and accountable human.
13. Ask at least one student to give the mission's 30-second AI-security
   interview answer in their own words.
14. Each student writes an individual career reflection of at least 100 words.
15. Confirm saving, then lock the mission for review.

The AI supervisor is intended to challenge reasoning, not provide an answer
key. Students must not paste real personal, employer, credential, or incident
data into an AI tool.

## Facilitate learning instead of task completion

Before class, tell students: “The goal is not to guess the hidden answer. Your
goal is to make the safest decision the current evidence can support, explain
what remains unknown, and change your mind when better reasoning requires it.”

While teams work, listen for these warning signs:

- one student speaks while the other roles merely agree;
- a conclusion is stated without an artifact number;
- a detection score or AI statement is treated as proof;
- the team recommends containment without discussing business impact;
- uncertainty is hidden to make the report sound confident; or
- students paste AI language they cannot explain aloud.

Use prompts instead of giving the answer:

- “Which artifact supports that statement?”
- “Is that a confirmed fact, a reasonable inference, or an unknown?”
- “What could make your conclusion wrong?”
- “What harm could occur if you act? What harm could occur if you wait?”
- “Who owns the next action?”
- “Explain that sentence without reading it.”

In the debrief, ask one team to defend its action and another to present the
strongest reasonable alternative. End by revisiting the five concepts and
asking students which one changed their decision. This turns the case into
retrieval practice, judgment practice, and professional communication—not an
AI-generated worksheet.

## Review work

A professor may review teams and private reflections only within the assigned
section. Check:

- whether claims cite specific artifacts;
- whether uncertainty is labeled honestly;
- whether containment is proportionate;
- whether all four roles contributed;
- whether the team completed the coaching check and can explain the feedback;
- whether the before-and-after decision identifies what changed and cites why;
- whether the shift briefing is concise, accurate, and assigns a next owner;
- whether the shared transcript reflects team reasoning; and
- whether each reflection explains an individual contribution, revised
  concept, and honest interview connection.

Record grades only in the approved course system.

## Classroom recovery

- Student forgot a password: do not ask for it. Follow the administrator's
  approved reset or replacement-account procedure.
- Student chose an identifying alias: stop and replace it with a neutral alias
  before more work is entered.
- Wrong student joined a team: use the professor team-management control to
  remove or move the student, then verify the roster before work continues.
- Work is not updating: wait for the save status, refresh once, and consult
  [Troubleshooting](TROUBLESHOOTING.md).
- Another section is visible: sign out immediately and report a security issue.

## End of mission

Confirm the shared report, AI transcript, and individual reflections are
complete. Lock the workspace, transfer assessment results to the approved
course system, and keep any instructor answer key outside the public
repository.
